## What it does
- Annotates an animal mitochondrial genome given as a FASTA file (first record only, treated as circular) with 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs and 13 protein-coding genes and writes GFF3, NCBI .tbl, rotated FASTA and SVG; HGNC-style names (MT-RNR1, MT-TF, MT-CO1) are used.
- Per feature class: tRNAs by Infernal `cmsearch` with 22 covariance models; rRNAs by HMMER `nhmmer` with two nucleotide profile HMMs (12S, 16S) and then snapping of the hit ends to neighbouring tRNAs; protein genes by translating all open reading frames of the six frames and searching them with 13 protein profile HMMs (`hmmsearch`), after which start and stop codons are chosen by a penalty scheme.
- The authors state it is "currently optimised for vertebrates, especially fish", with the intention of broader metazoan use; the model artifact is named `Emma_vertebrate_models`.
- One command: `julia --project=<Emma> <Emma>/src/command.jl [--invertebrates] [--rotate MT-TM] --gff x.gff genome.fasta`; multiple files in parallel with `--threads`.

## Algorithm & key source locations
- Order of work per genome: tRNAs first, then rRNAs (which need the tRNAs), then protein genes (which also need the tRNAs); the genome is made circular and 100 nt of the start are appended before every search so that features crossing the origin are found (Emma/src/process.jl:97) (Emma/src/process.jl:105-175) (Emma/src/circularity.jl:76-83)
- rRNA models: two nucleotide profile HMMs in one file, `12srna` (1299 consensus columns, NSEQ 1955, EFFN 18.66, built 2022-10-04) and `16srna` (1613 columns, NSEQ 2246, EFFN 7.79, built 2022-10-05), HMMER 3.3.2 format, `STATS LOCAL` lines present; they are profile HMMs, not covariance models (emma-models/rrn/12S.hmm:1-16) (emma-models/rrn/all_rrn.hmm:3921-3937) (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:8)
- The HMM headers carry no `COM` build line and no taxon information; the models repository has no README or build script, so the training sequences, their taxa and the alignment method are 未确证; the low effective sequence numbers (18.7 and 7.8 of 1955 and 2246) suggest highly redundant training sets [inference] (emma-models/rrn/12S.hmm:1-17) (https://github.com/ian-small/emma-models)
- History of the rRNA models: the first design (alpha, 2022) used 3'-end covariance models `endrrns.cm` (70 columns, 27 sequences) and `endrrnl.cm` (88 columns, 28 sequences) plus two tiny 5'-end HMMs (`1-169.hmm`, `1-70.hmm`, 2 sequences each) and took the end positions from them; v1.0.0 (code 2024-07-25, models 2024-07-24) deleted these and replaced them by the two full-length HMMs plus tRNA snapping; the authors give no reason (https://github.com/ian-small/emma-models/commit/bfa1a72491c2af553db3d2b69a062bfbdb9386e4) (https://github.com/ian-small/Emma/commit/80fd96d32520ed1547eb36475bcbe825664e6d02) (https://github.com/ian-small/Emma/blob/b1ed512/src/rRNAs.jl)
- rRNA search command: `nhmmer --tblout <tbl> all_rrn.hmm extended.fa`, no other option, so HMMER defaults apply (E-value reporting threshold 10, both strands, heuristic filters on) (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:7-16) (knowledge/experiments/emma_hmm/env_check.txt:12-16)
- rRNA hit parsing: the alignment coordinates `ali from/to` (columns 7-8) are used, not the envelope; model coordinates are columns 5-6; the strand and the E-value (column 13) are kept; reverse-strand hits are converted to 5'-to-3' coordinates of their own strand (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:18-36)
- rRNA hit clean-up (shared with protein genes): when two or more hits exist, hits with E-value >= 1e-5 are dropped; for two hits of different models on the same strand that overlap by more than half of the shorter one the worse E-value is deleted; for two hits of the same model, the 12S/16S models skip the reading-frame test and the hits are merged when they overlap or when the gap between them in the genome matches the gap between them in the model within sqrt(0.1) of the model span ((gap_genome - gap_model)^2 < 0.1 x span^2), otherwise the worse hit is deleted; the merged hit runs from the first start to the last end with the better E-value (Emma/src/feature.jl:49-86) (Emma/src/feature.jl:31-34)
- Consequence of that clean-up: after it at most one hit per model remains, i.e. at most one 12S and one 16S (Emma/src/feature.jl:69-83)
- E-value cut for rRNAs: only hits with E-value < 1e-10 are kept after clean-up; there is no fallback if a gene has no hit (Emma/src/process.jl:139-141)
- rRNA end setting (`fix_rrn_ends!`), done on the strand of the rRNA with the same-strand tRNA list: 5' end = if the nearest preceding tRNA ends fewer than 100 nt before the hit start (or overlaps it) the start is moved to the first nucleotide after that tRNA; 3' end = pick the tRNA whose end is closest to the hit end, and if that tRNA's start is within 50 nt of the hit end (gap or overlap) the end is moved to the nucleotide before that tRNA's start; otherwise the HMM alignment coordinate stays (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:44-75) (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:38-42)
- The gene order tRNA-Phe, rrnS, tRNA-Val, rrnL, tRNA-Leu is not hard-coded: any same-strand tRNA is used as the neighbour, and no check ties 12S to Phe/Val or 16S to Val/Leu; there is no rule for an rRNA next to a protein gene or the control region, and no rRNA-versus-CDS conflict resolution (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:44-75) (Emma/src/process.jl:139-175)
- tRNA models: 22 covariance models `trn*_fish` (CLEN 66-74), described in the code as "Pavel's fish-specific models", built in 2023 with `cmbuild` from alignments of 1883-6325 sequences; four were built from `refseq_models/*.sto`, one from `Fishali/trnH.sto`; two models for Leu and two for Ser (L1/L2, S1/S2); a serialized Julia dictionary gives the anticodon position of each model (Emma/src/tRNAs.jl:35-42) (emma-models/trn/trnF_fish.cm:2-22) (emma-models/trn/all_trn.cm:554) (emma-models/trn/all_trn.cm:3271)
- tRNA search: `cmsearch <all_trn.cm> extended.fa` with no option (Infernal defaults: local alignment, E-value reporting limit 10); the first hit row of each `>>` alignment block is parsed and kept if E-value <= 1e-5; the gene identity is taken from the anticodon read from the alignment at the model's anticodon position (22 anticodons listed, so Leu/Ser copies are named by anticodon), not from the model name (Emma/src/tRNAs.jl:160-168) (Emma/src/tRNAs.jl:114-158) (Emma/src/tRNAs.jl:7-10) (knowledge/experiments/emma_hmm/env_check.txt:18-20)
- tRNA clean-up: only one hit per tRNA identity is kept (the lower E-value); same-strand hits that overlap by 5 nt or more keep the better one; tRNAs that still overlap a neighbour are cut at the neighbour start, 10 A residues are appended and the model is searched again, and the result replaces the original if its E-value is better (tRNA completed by polyadenylation, recorded in the GFF note) (Emma/src/tRNAs.jl:100-112) (Emma/src/tRNAs.jl:48-68) (Emma/src/process.jl:111-132) (Emma/src/gff.jl:62-68)
- Protein genes: every ORF of at least 150 nt from a permitted start codon to the next stop codon, on both strands, is translated (first residue forced to Met) and searched with `hmmsearch --domtblout` against 13 protein HMMs (one per gene, built from 430 to 3968 sequences); domain hits with independent E-value <= 1 are accepted, then cleaned up with the rule above (Emma/src/process.jl:3) (Emma/src/orfs.jl:47-91) (Emma/src/orfs.jl:120-149) (emma-models/cds/all_cds.hmm:925-934)
- Protein-gene boundaries: the HMM hit only fixes the approximate start; the real start is chosen among in-frame start codons lying up to the smaller of (distance to the preceding tRNA, distance to the preceding in-frame stop) upstream and 50 nt downstream of the hit start, by maximising a penalty score (30 x codon penalty with ATG 0, GTG -3.4, CTG -7.2, TTG -7.9, ATA -8.2, ATT -9.5, ATC -10; -100 per nt for a start downstream of the hit; -20 per nt for a start upstream of the hit; -50 x (rank - 1) for starts within 30 nt of a preceding tRNA, ranked by distance from it); the stop is the first in-frame stop after the start, with stop positions added at tRNA starts so that a gene ending in a truncated stop is cut there (Emma/src/orfs.jl:155-248) (Emma/src/process.jl:19-31) (Emma/src/orfs.jl:5-9) (Emma/src/orfs.jl:151-153)
- Starts inside a tRNA are removed; the HMM hit end is not used for the stop; a stop codon that overlaps the next tRNA is written as completed by 3' A residues (Emma/src/process.jl:6-17) (Emma/src/orfs.jl:233-243) (Emma/src/gff.jl:49-57)
- ND3 frameshift: two hits of the same gene are joined and split again at a hard-coded motif `.TT.CT.AGTAGC` (conserved ND3 slippage site), written as "ribosomal slippage" (Emma/src/feature.jl:134-146) (Emma/src/gb.jl:31-37)
- `--rotate <feature>` rotates the sequence and the annotation to start at the first feature whose attribute contains the given text (e.g. MT-TM), reverse-complementing the genome if that feature is on the minus strand (Emma/src/process.jl:50-76) (Emma/README.md:56-58)

## Defaults that matter
- Genetic code: vertebrate mitochondrial table 2 by default; `--invertebrates` (`-i`) switches to table 5 and changes nothing else (models, rRNA and tRNA steps are identical) (Emma/src/cmd.jl:16-17) (Emma/src/cmd.jl:83)

## Relevance to our pipeline
- What is transferable: EMMA's rRNA method is "sensitive local profile search, merge fragments, snap ends to the flanking same-strand tRNA within 100/50 nt", which is the same family as our `mito_curate.py` R1 trim/fill (max_fill 300) on top of MitoZ's Infernal CMs; the new elements are (a) detection by nucleotide profile HMMs with `nhmmer`, (b) a rule that merges fragmented hits by checking the gap against the model gap, (c) tighter and asymmetric snapping windows [inference] (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:44-75) (mito_curate.py:279-319)
- What is not transferable as shipped: the two rRNA HMMs and 22 tRNA CMs; the probe shows they find 1 of 46 rRNAs in the eight target phyla (the MITOS2 CMs already find 24 of 46), so using EMMA as is would not repair our misses and would be a regression against the CMs we have; the invertebrate switch only changes the translation table. The 22 features that both model sets miss (Nematoda 7, Placozoa 5, Ctenophora 4, Kinorhyncha 2, Xenacoelomorpha 2, Gnathostomulida 1, Tardigrada 1) are the real target of any adaptation [inference from our run] (knowledge/experiments/emma_hmm/result_emma_hmm_dev.txt:65-95) (knowledge/experiments/emma_hmm/compare_hmm_cm.txt:2-9) (Emma/src/cmd.jl:83)
- Idea 1 (per-clade detection models): build nucleotide profile HMMs for rrnS and rrnL per phylum (or per group of related phyla: e.g. Nematoda; Chaetognatha; Ctenophora+Cnidaria; Platyhelminthes+Acoela+Gnathostomulida+Kinorhyncha+Tardigrada where sequences allow) from NCBI/RefSeq rRNA features (our `fetch_panel.py` already pulls them per clade), align them to the MITOS2 CM with `cmalign` (as in our `pal_cols.py` work), then `hmmbuild` as EMMA presumably did (EMMA's own build method is 未确证). Test recall with leave-one-genus-out so a genome's own rRNA is never in its model; success = detection (>= 50 % coverage) of some of the 22 target-phylum features that the MITOS2 CMs miss, without losing the 24 they find. Our earlier negative result for clade-specific CMs concerned ends, not detection, so this is still open [inference] (knowledge/experiments/rrna_ends/clade_cm_vs_universal_dev.txt:1-12)
- Idea 2 (two-stage search, detection then ends): relaxing the threshold alone helps little (EMMA HMMs 83 features at 1e-10, 87 at 1e-5, 93 at E < 10; MITOS2 CMs 159, 164, 166; target phyla 24, 27, 29 of 46 for the CMs, so the limit is the model, not the cut-off); instead run a sensitive detector (profile HMM or CM) only on the unannotated gaps between already called tRNAs and PCGs, require plausible lengths (clade windows, Idea 2 in `knowledge/topics/rrna_annotation_methods.md`) and the rrnS/rrnL order, then set ends with R1 snapping. This uses information EMMA does not use (the PCG calls) and targets genomes where the rRNA is simply "the big gap" [inference] (knowledge/experiments/emma_hmm/compare_hmm_cm.txt:3-7) (knowledge/topics/rrna_annotation_methods.md:152-153)
- Idea 3 (fragment merging): port EMMA's rule (same model, same strand, merge if gap in genome agrees with gap in model within sqrt(0.1) x span, hit span first start to last end) to repair partial profile hits; in the probe the rrnL features of Platyhelminthes, Placozoa, Kinorhyncha and Tardigrada have median coverage 0.24 to 0.43 with 1 to 2 hits per genome at E < 1e-10, the pattern merging would address (whether the hits are adjacent pieces of one gene was not checked, 未确证). Test on the dev set: coverage and both-ends-within-3 before and after merging [inference] (Emma/src/feature.jl:70-82) (knowledge/experiments/emma_hmm/result_emma_hmm_dev.txt:34-64)
- Idea 4 (snapping windows): EMMA uses 100 nt for the 5' end and 50 nt for the 3' end, ours uses 300 nt on both; replay `rule_sim.py` with 100/50 and 100/100 to see whether asymmetry helps (our earlier replay: fill <= 30 loses 6 rrnS ends, 300 is better than 30); do not copy EMMA's second 5' condition (see Known bugs) [inference] (Emma/src/rRNAs.jl:54-65) (knowledge/topics/rrna_annotation_methods.md:164-166)
- Idea 5 (terminal models): EMMA's authors started with short end CMs/HMMs (alpha) and removed them by v1.0.0; with our finding that terminal motifs are weak outside vertebrate/insect rrnS 5' ends, this route is low priority [inference] (https://github.com/ian-small/emma-models/commit/bfa1a72491c2af553db3d2b69a062bfbdb9386e4) (knowledge/topics/rrna_annotation_methods.md:102) (knowledge/topics/rrna_annotation_methods.md:171)
- tRNA step ideas: the "cut at the next tRNA and re-search with 10 A appended" trick for tRNAs whose 3' end is completed by polyadenylation is an inexpensive addition to `mito_trna.py` for overlapped tRNAs; EMMA's one-gene-per-identity rule and fish CMs are not suitable for invertebrates [inference] (Emma/src/process.jl:115-132) (Emma/src/tRNAs.jl:100-112)
- PCG step ideas: the start-choice penalty scheme (codon penalty x 30, -100 per nt for starts downstream of the HMM start, -20 per nt for starts upstream, window 50 nt downstream, stop set truncated at tRNAs) can be compared with the MITOS2/MitoZ rules listed in `knowledge/topics/pcg_boundary_methods.md`; for table 5 it already excludes CTG; no benchmark exists for invertebrates, so it would need our dev set [inference] (Emma/src/orfs.jl:155-248) (knowledge/topics/pcg_boundary_methods.md:1)
- Cost to run EMMA itself as a baseline: Julia must be installed (not present), package resolution is unpinned, and the invertebrate switch gives only the table-5 codon sets; for the eight target phyla the probe already predicts misses, so installing it only makes sense for a vertebrate control (e.g. fish) [inference] (knowledge/experiments/emma_hmm/env_check.txt:2) (Emma/Project.toml:6-17)
